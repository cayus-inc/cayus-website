Design a logo mark for "Cayus," a premium B2B recruitment agency in the US that books
qualified candidate interviews for hiring companies. The brand should feel discreet,
serious, and trustworthy -- closer to a high-end consultancy or a precision instrument
brand than a consumer app or a dated corporate firm.

NAME MEANING (use as creative inspiration, not literally): Cayus comes from Caius/Gaius
Octavius, the birth name of Augustus, Rome's first emperor, before he took the name
"Augustus" at 35. Augustus ended a century of civil war through discipline and quiet
authority, not conquest or spectacle. The brand should echo precision and restraint, not
grandeur or ornamentation.

STRICT PALETTE: warm near-black ink (#0D0D0C), warm near-white paper (#FAF8F4), and one
desaturated metallic accent -- pewter (#9A9488, lighter #C7C0B2, darker #6B6559). No
saturated or vivid colors of any kind. The mark should work as a single flat color (pure
ink on paper, or pure paper on ink) as well as with the pewter gradient as an accent.

STYLE DIRECTION, based on real reference logos I'm attaching (all from one branding
studio's portfolio, do not copy them, use them as a quality and style bar): the marks
that work are simple, deliberate, abstract geometric constructions -- arcs, chevrons,
bars, circles, triangles, combined with precision. Look especially at the Softriver mark
itself (two curved chevrons forming an abstract "S"), the Monty mark (a minimal "//."
glyph), the Exposure mark (four vertical bars of varying height), and the Atlas mark (a
mountain/peak "A" abstraction in a classical, sober register). Notice none of these are
organic, wavy, or scalloped shapes -- they're all built from a small number of precise
geometric primitives.

WHAT NOT TO DO, from things I already tried and rejected: no hand-drawn or organic blob
shapes, no scalloped/wavy medallion or flower-like outlines (tried this, it read as "a
cookie" or "an old star," not premium), no generic rounded-square-plus-icon startup logo
cliche, no literal serif letter dropped inside a plain circle badge.

WHAT IT SHOULD DO: evoke the initial "C" for Cayus, or an abstract form that echoes it,
without being a literal typeset letter in a bubble. It needs to read clearly at very
small sizes (a 16px browser favicon) and hold up large (a 1200px social share image). It
should look at home next to Stripe, Linear, or Vercel in terms of restraint and
precision, but with a slightly more classical, grounded weight given the Roman naming
story -- not futuristic, not playful.

You have full creative freedom on the exact concept (an arc, a chevron, a negative-space
cut, an abstract monogram, or something else entirely) as long as it is geometric,
deliberately constructed, simple, and fits the palette and tone above. Please generate a
few distinct directions rather than one single option.
